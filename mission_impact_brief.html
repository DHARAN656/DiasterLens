Mission Impact Brief

Track 03 — Multimodal Geospatial Workloads · April 26, 2025

The Problem

After a major disaster, emergency managers must produce a structure-level damage inventory before federal aid flows. Today that takes 2–3 days — filed by hand across isolated teams that never compare notes. Drone footage, satellite imagery, and field reports live in separate silos. Silent missed properties never appear in the federal package. Survivors wait.

End User & Use Case
Who
State / Local / Tribal / Territorial Emergency Manager (SLTT EM)
Workflow
FEMA Preliminary Damage Assessment (PDA) — gating step for Presidential disaster declaration
When
Hour 4–12 post-impact, before Joint PDA field teams mobilise
Inputs available
UAS drone video · Esri satellite tiles · Field reports · County parcel CSV
Real Events Validated

Tested on Hurricane Milton (FL, Oct 2024) — $34 B damage, county inventory took weeks — and Palisades Wildfire (CA, Jan 2025) — 6,800+ structures destroyed, parcel tagging ran through February 2025. Both are exact use-cases where DisasterLens cuts the critical path.

Comparison Baseline

Baseline: manual cross-referencing by a GIS analyst + building inspector team. DisasterLens matched or exceeded baseline on all 5 hand-labeled sites: 100% precision · 100% recall · 1 of 1 conflict correctly flagged. Throughput: 6 videos + 4 satellite comparisons processed in ~45 min on Bedrock.

Honest Limitations
  • Geo-location relies on visible landmarks; GPS-tagged video removes this dependency in production
  • Pre-event satellite baseline (Esri Wayback 2014) is ~10 yr old; same-month imagery reduces false detections
  • Address fuzzy matching fails on informal names (PCH vs Pacific Coast Highway); geocoding normalisation required
  • Field report quality is the weakest real-world link — unstructured PDFs need pre-processing
