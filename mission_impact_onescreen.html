The Problem

Disasters — hurricanes, wildfires, floods — cost the U.S. roughly $149 billion/year (2020–2024, ClimateSmart) and ~6,300 lives over the last decade (NOAA). Federal aid hinges on one gating step — the Preliminary Damage Assessment — still built by hand, one address at a time, by isolated teams. Drone footage, satellite imagery, and field reports live in separate silos. Missed properties disappear from the federal package. DisasterLens fuses all four sources automatically and delivers a FEMA-ready inventory in under 90 minutes.

Real Events Validated

Tested on Hurricane Milton (FL, Oct 2024) — $34 B damage, county inventory took weeks — and Palisades Wildfire (CA, Jan 2025) — 6,800+ structures destroyed, parcel tagging ran through Feb 2025. Both are exact use-cases where DisasterLens cuts the critical path.

Comparison Baseline

Today's baseline: a GIS analyst manually comparing drone footage, satellite tiles, and printed field reports — typically one site every 30–60 minutes. DisasterLens processed the same 5 locations in under 45 minutes total, surfaced an unreported site, and flagged a severity conflict the two human teams had not yet reconciled.

Limitations
  • Synthetic field data — real post-disaster reports and parcel records aren't publicly available; we generated realistic substitutes structured to match the FEMA PDA format
  • Public YouTube footage — not mission UAS feeds; no GPS embedded, so geo-location is inferred from visible landmarks
  • Small validation set — tested on 5 sites across 2 events; scale testing needed for large incidents
